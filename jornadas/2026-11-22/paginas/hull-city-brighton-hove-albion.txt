FLASHSCORE PREVIEW

Hull face a considerable challenge as they look to end a long Premier League winless run as high-flying Brighton make the long journey north for a first-ever top-flight meeting.

Match News and Current Form

An incredible start to their Premier League return has now officially faded for Hull, whose 1-0 loss at Arsenal before the international break made them just the fourth Premier League side to ever win their first two matches of a season then fail to win any of their next eight. Ominously, the other three - including the Tigers themselves in 2016/17 - were all relegated, and given that Sergej Jakirović’s side start the round just three points clear of the relegation zone, anxiety will likely be widespread in the MKM Stadium. Hull are usually more comfortable here though, last losing on their own turf back in March (W3, D7).

Brighton must fancy their chances of ending that run as they start the round as Arsenal’s closest challengers at the Premier League summit. The Seagulls have won six of their last seven league fixtures (L1), keeping a clean sheet in each victory, helping them equal an all-time English top-flight record of seven wins to nil in a side’s first ten games of a campaign. Fabian Hürzeler’s men have been superb frontrunners, leading in a division-high 54.3% of their matches, though all three of their defeats in all competitions this term coming on the road indicates they can take nothing for granted here.

Head-to-Head History

These sides haven’t faced each other since February 2016. Brighton are yet to win a game in any competition at the MKM Stadium (D2, L4) and last won away at Hull in October 2000.

Hot Stats and Streaks

Hull have attempted a league-low 103 shots so far this season.

Hull’s last seven league matches produced over 8.5 corners.

Brighton have conceded just once after half-time in the Premier League this term.

A league-low one of Brighton’s league fixtures this season stood level at half-time (HT: W6, L3).

Key Players to Watch and Missing Players

Konstantinos Tzolakis made a league-high 41 saves across the first ten rounds, nine of which came in Hull’s last match. No German player in Premier League history has directly contributed to more goals than Pascal Groß (87), who starts the round having created a league-high 29 chances.

Hull hope to have Mohamed Belloumi available again here but saw Nobel Mendy pull out of international duty through injury. Pascal Struijk remains doubtful for Brighton.
