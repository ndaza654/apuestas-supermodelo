FLASHSCORE PREVIEW

It’s getting close to crunch time for Feyenoord and Porto in the UEFA Champions League (UCL) after both clubs spent the halfway point of the league phase outside the top 24. 

Match News and Current Form

The UCL was always likely to be difficult for a Feyenoord side that struggled in last season’s Europa League (32nd) and, after being outclassed by Inter Milan (3-0) in their last UCL fixture, they sit a point adrift of the top 24. Feyenoord have now lost nine of their last 12 continental games (W3), and although all three wins came at home, they find themselves six games without victory in all competitions (D2, L4) after a surprise weekend loss at home to Excelsior. It’s difficult to see that run ending due to the rate at which Feyenoord concede goals in the UCL, as they have already picked the ball out of the back of their net 11 times in this league phase and kept just two clean sheets across their last 21 matches in this competition proper.

Porto performed much better than Feyenoord in last season's Europa League but have also found the transition to the UCL difficult, though there appear to be signs of a strong resurgence. Francesco Farioli's side thrashed Napoli 4-1 at home on matchday four to earn their first points of the league phase, and whilst they needed penalties to get past third-tier club Paredes in a weekend cup game, victory stretched their winning run to six games in all competitions. Losing their last three away games in the UCL proper offers a huge note of caution here though and leaves them at risk of four straight defeats in such matches for the first time since November 1998.

Head-to-Head History

These clubs traded home wins in the 2019/20 Europa League as part of Feyenoord’s strong record at home to Portuguese sides in major European competition (W3, D3, L1). Porto, meanwhile, are without a win in four away matches against Dutch clubs (D2, L2).

Hot Stats and Streaks

Feyenoord's last 15 home games in the UCL produced a first-half goal.

Feyenoord conceded the opening goal in all four UCL matches this season.

Eleven of the 13 goals in Porto’s league phase games this term came in the second half. 

Five of Porto’s last seven European away fixtures produced under 2.5 goals. 

Key Players to Watch and Missing Players

Feyenoord will be hoping Gjivai Zechiël can break his drought here given four of his five goalscoring appearances this season ended in victory, whilst four saw him score Feyenoord’s opening goal. Porto’s Samu was rested at the weekend and could feature prominently in this match having scored each of his last two competitive goals after half-time in the UCL.

Feyenoord are hopeful that Anis Hadj Moussa can recover in time for this match, whilst Porto’s William Gomes remains doubtful as he closes in on a long-awaited return.

With the home side’s slump and the visitors’ winning streak in mind, backing Porto to win seems the obvious choice.
