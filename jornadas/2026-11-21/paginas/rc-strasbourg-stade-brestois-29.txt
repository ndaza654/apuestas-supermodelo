After trading the English second tier for a side in the bottom half of Ligue 1, Vítor Matos begins his Strasbourg managerial era against an in-form Brest.

Match News and Current Form

A 3-1 defeat at Toulouse in their last game was Strasbourg’s fifth loss in six Ligue 1 matches (W1) - matching their tally from their previous 23 such fixtures (W10, D8) - and ultimately spelt the end for Hugo Oliveira. Matos arrives from Swansea to replace him in the dugout and his first job is to shore up the division’s joint-worst defensive record after ten rounds, with a whopping 22 goals conceded contributing to Le Racing sitting only two points above the relegation zone pre-round. In his favour is Strasbourg’s form at home, as they’ve lost just one of their last four games at Stade de la Meinau (W2, D1), thrashing Auxerre 5-1 in their last match here.

Brest opened the season with just two wins in their first eight Ligue 1 outings (D3, L3), but successive 2-0 victories over Lorient and Lyon catapulted them into the top half and within striking distance of the European places. Those two wins are as many as they managed in their prior 17 games in the league (D6, L9), none of which saw the Pirates keep a clean sheet. Brest now go in search of a third successive shutout and are also targeting back-to-back top flight victories on the road for the first time since February 2025.

Head-to-Head History

The away side on the day won both H2Hs last season, with Brest’s win in this fixture their third in their last four trips to Strasbourg (D1), with their last loss here coming back in August 2021.

Hot Stats and Streaks

Strasbourg have failed to keep a clean sheet in their last 18 Ligue 1 games.

Strasbourg’s last six home league matches have seen both teams score.

Brest have scored the opening goal in a pre-round league-high eight games this season.

Brest haven't been behind at half-time in any of their last six away matches (HT: W3, D3).

Key Players to Watch and Missing Players

Gessime Yassine contributed to three of Strasbourg’s five goals in their last home game (G2, A1) and all four of his goals this term came in the second half of home victories. Ludovic Ajorque faces a side he represented a career-high 151 times, and will be confident of haunting his former employers having scored in each of his last three matches, while he was also on target in the last H2H.

Strasbourg have doubts over Filip Jörgensen and Giovanni Reyna, while Brest appear to have no new availability issues.
