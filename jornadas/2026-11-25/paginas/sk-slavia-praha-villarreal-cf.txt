FLASHSCORE PREVIEW

The struggling pair of Slavia Prague and Villarreal will see this meeting as a golden opportunity to improve their chances of qualifying for the UEFA Champions League (UCL) knockouts.

Match News and Current Form

Slavia’s 19-year wait for a UCL main draw victory continues after their 2-1 defeat to Arsenal left them languishing near the bottom of the standings (D1, L3). The Red and Whites have recorded a Czech record of 19 consecutive European games without winning (D6, L13), and with trips to Bayern Munich and Porto ahead of them, they badly need to take their superb domestic form into European football. Slavia scored an impressive 15 goals in their last four domestic games (W4), and although they’ve recently been competitive at home in the UCL, losing both home fixtures this term by one-goal margins, they’re now on a club record run of ten consecutive home European matches without victory (D4, L6).

Villarreal’s 6-0 defeat to PSG on matchday four left them outside the top-24 with just three points (W1, L3), whilst their defensive frailties were again on display at the weekend when they squandered an early 2-0 lead to lose 3-2 to Barcelona. That has been a common theme throughout their recent European journeys, given Villarreal have already scored eight UCL goals this term - three more than they managed in the entirety of the league phase last season - but have now lost 12 of their last 14 games in this competition proper (W1, D1). That includes defeat in each of their last seven on the road, a record for any Spanish club.

Head-to-Head History

Villarreal are unbeaten across the two previous H2Hs (W1, D1), winning 2-0 here in 2017/18.

Hot Stats and Streaks

Slavia conceded at least two goals in each of their last seven UCL fixtures.

Nine of Slavia’s last 11 home games in all competitions produced over 2.5 total goals.

Villarreal have conceded in each of their last 15 UCL fixtures.

There was at least one goal scored after the 85th minute in three of Villarreal’s last four away UCL matches.

Key Players to Watch and Missing Players

Mojmír Chytil scored in the opening five minutes of both of Slavia’s last two domestic fixtures and all five of his goalscoring outings this term saw him net his club’s first of the game. On the other hand, Georges Mikautadze scored in two of his last three UCL outings and three of his five competitive goals this season came after the 75th minute.

Slavia have injury concerns over Štěpán Chaloupek, Lukáš Provod and Danijel Šturm, while David Moses suffered an injury during Saturday’s 2-0 win over Sigma Olomouc. Juan Foyth and Gerard Moreno are among Villarreal’s likely absentees.
